Overview

Animals need regular food after their first player feeding, and newborns start eating automatically. Hunger ranges from 0 (fully fed) to 1 (starving), reduces movement speed, and can cause damage. Feeding lowers hunger instead of immediately causing breeding; well-fed adults periodically enter love mode and need a compatible partner.

Checking hunger

Right-click an affected animal with an empty main hand. It plays its own ambient sound and a single status particle rises from eye height plus 0.25 blocks. Nearby players can see/hear the feedback. Silent animals remain silent, and animals without an ambient sound do not invent one. Normal mounting, sitting and other empty-hand actions still work. Excluded animals and spectators receive no feedback.

A check never starts tracking or feeds the animal. Activated animals first catch up hunger to the current world tick; starvation damage is never caught up. Unfed animals show a neutral sparkle and the optional label "not being tracked yet", rather than a misleading full heart.

Feeding and hunger

All Animal mobs are eligible, including modded animals using vanilla food and breeding behavior. Before first feeding, wild adults do not gain hunger, slow down, take starvation damage or breed. They can still follow food. Newborns start tracked and fully fed automatically. Exclusions retain all vanilla behavior.

The first accepted meal begins at initialHunger and then subtracts (nutrition + absolute saturation) * foodModifier, clamped to 0–1. Subsequent meals use current hunger. Food without vanilla nutrition, such as wheat or seeds, uses fallbackNutrition and fallbackSaturation. For example, default wheat removes 0.05 hunger. Food is refused strictly below refuseFoodBelow, except the first meal. Feeding consumes one item outside Creative mode; tropical fish buckets return a water bucket.

Horses can eat wheat, sugar, hay, apples and their breeding foods. Parrots can eat their usual seeds even though they cannot breed. Vanilla untamed cat/parrot feeding also attempts taming; wolf bones tame as usual and do not activate hunger. Hunger feeding of tamed animals replaces vanilla food healing and baby growth boosts.

Hunger increases by hungerPerTick per world tick. Before care bonuses, default adult hunger goes from 0 to 1 in about one Minecraft day (20 minutes). Unloaded chunks catch up when the animal next ticks or is fed, adding at most maxHungerCatchUp (0.5) hunger per elapsed interval. Server shutdown time does not count. Starvation hits begin only after starvationIntervalTicks loaded ticks at or above starvationThreshold; reloading starts a fresh timer, with no accumulated damage.

Movement multiplier is 1 - hunger * (1 - minSpeedMultiplier). The default is normal speed at 0 hunger and half speed at 1. It stacks multiplicatively with other attribute modifiers and is removed when disabled or excluded.

Hunger rate modifiers

Babies gain hunger 25% faster by default. Set babyHungerRateEnabled = false to give them the ordinary rate, or change babyHungerMultiplier (default 1.25). The baby multiplier stops when the animal matures; feeding still does not accelerate baby growth.

Optional individual appetite variation starts off. Enable individualHungerRateEnabled to give each activated animal a randomly chosen multiplier between individualHungerMultiplierMin and individualHungerMultiplierMax (default 0.8 and 1.2). Rolls are independent per animal and saved with it. Reversed bounds are swapped. Disabling variation ignores the roll without deleting it; re-enabling restores it. Changing the bounds affects newly assigned animals rather than rerolling existing ones. Animals already tracked before enabling receive a roll on their next hunger update. First feeding assigns a roll when variation is enabled; inspection alone never activates an animal.

Effective hunger gain is (hungerPerTick * baby multiplier * individual multiplier * matching datapack multipliers) / (1 + care level * careHungerReductionPerLevel), followed by any API rate adjustment. Baby/individual factors apply only when enabled. Pigs have a shipped 1.1 datapack multiplier. See Datapacks for entity, tag and regex selectors. The final rate is capped at 1 hunger per tick. Both modifiers also apply during chunk catch-up. Catch-up uses current age/config; vanilla age does not progress while the animal is unloaded. For example, a baby with the default 1.25 baby multiplier and a 1.2 individual roll gets hungry 1.5 times as fast as the base rate. Zero multipliers pause hunger gain; existing starvation damage settings still apply.

Grass and recovery

Activated animals that finish naturally eating grass recover 0.05 hunger by default. This covers sheep-style block eating and completed horse grazing, preserving vanilla wool/growth effects. Interrupted horse grazing gives no relief. Wild animals do not start hunger tracking from grass. No new grass-eating AI is added to cows, pigs or other animals; custom mods can report grazing through the API.

When hunger actually drops, it rests for 40 world ticks (2 seconds) before increasing again. This applies to feeding, grass and API reductions, and is saved across reloads. Catch-up includes only time after the recovery deadline. New reductions restart the pause; unchanged hunger does not extend it. Set hungerRecoveryDelayTicks = 0 to disable the pause.

Feedback after feeding

Every successful meal shows one rising status particle for the hunger after the food is applied, using the same stages as empty-hand inspection. A meal that makes the animal too full for more food shows a heart. Refused food gives no feeding feedback. Untamed cat/parrot feeding completes its vanilla taming/food-consumption interaction before showing the result.

Set showFeedingActionBar = true to also show the updated status to the feeding player. This starts false and is independent of showHungerActionBar. Both use the configured stage labels and hungerActionBarFormat. Feeding feedback does not play a second inspection ambient sound.

Natural breeding

Feeding never directly starts love mode for affected animals, including the first feeding. Every breedingCheckTicks loaded ticks, an activated adult below breedingThreshold has breedingChance to enter vanilla love mode. Vanilla compatibility, taming, health, partner and pregnancy/egg requirements still apply. A fed animal cannot naturally breed with an unactivated animal, since the latter cannot enter love mode.

Each parent records world time when vanilla finalizes offspring, or frog/turtle pregnancy. A random inclusive min/max breeding delay is saved independently per parent. Reversed bounds are swapped. The configured delay replaces vanilla's adult age cooldown; its elapsed time continues while unloaded, but missed breeding rolls do not accumulate. An animal leaves love mode if its hunger rises to the threshold. Newborns start tracked and fully fed. Unfed wild adults cannot breed or mate.

Care levels and first feeding

Tracked animals earn care experience during loaded ticks when hunger is below 0.25 by default. Every 24000 experience gives a level, up to 10. Hunger gain is divided by 1 + level × 0.1, so level 10 halves appetite. Experience persists; lowering the cap retains it. Unloaded time awards no care. The first meal that activates a wild adult sends a one-time happy-villager burst around it. Newborns activate fully fed without a first meal or this burst.

Hunger catch-up adds at most 0.5 hunger per elapsed interval by default, rather than limiting total hunger to 0.5. Excess elapsed time is consumed once; loaded tick gain is unchanged.

Upgrading from FFAnimalsEat

Replace the old jar on both client and server. Existing saved animal hunger, breeding cooldowns and individual appetite are read automatically. Rename config/ffanimalseat-common.toml to config/ffanimalcare-common.toml to retain settings. Update custom datapack directories and resource IDs to ffanimalcare; developer integrations use the renamed package and API version 2. Do not keep both jars installed.

Settings live in config/ffanimalcare-common.toml. Keep client/server settings consistent; these common settings are not automatically synchronized. Twenty ticks are one second at normal server speed. Thresholds and hunger values range from 0 to 1.

Compatibility and datapacks

Required on both server and client because hunger and activation use tracked entity data. FFUtilities 0.2.14 is bundled; Fabric also requires Fabric API. Modded Animal subclasses inherit hooks, but custom feeding, breeding or temptation implementations may bypass them. Exclude those entities if needed. There are no special named mod integrations. Hunger-rate rules are described on Datapacks; portable compatibility callbacks and custom feeding/grazing/breeding helpers are described on Modding API. Vanilla animal food tags remain available where Minecraft uses them.
