FFEntityDirt

Modrinth ↗Source ↗

Overview

Entities pick up dirt from the ground, soot from fire and explosions, snow in snowfall and damage marks where they are hit, and wash it all off in water, rain or a cauldron bath.

Features

  • Moving on natural ground (soil, sand, gravel, clay, mud, snow, stone) leaves dirt low on the body in the color of the block underneath; floors like planks and carpet stay clean. Faster movement means more dirt that reaches higher, with a configurable spread that flings stray flecks further up; standing still adds none. Each time dirt lands, a small puff of the ground's particles kicks up (can be turned off).
  • Breaking a block sometimes flings specks of its color onto the player, on the side facing the block.
  • Fall damage splatters dirt from the bottom up, scaled by the damage taken.
  • Fire damage leaves soot, heaviest at the feet and fading smoothly toward the head. Explosion damage leaves soot on the side facing the blast, darker the closer it went off.
  • Hits leave wounds that match the weapon: swords slash, axes gash, spears and daggers stab, tridents leave three holes, maces and hammers crush, fists and shovels bruise, and wolves, cats, bees and hoglins bite, claw, sting and gore. Melee wounds land where the attacker was looking (a hit to the head marks the head), bigger when the attacker is close and thicker when the hit is hard, and go on a little unevenly so they look messy rather than stamped. Modded weapons are recognized through tags, Better Combat and Epic Fight weapon types and their names. With Epic Fight, wounds land where the swinging weapon met the body, every mob a sweep passes through is marked, and an off-hand weapon leaves its own wound. Each mob or tag can have its own color.
  • Damage marks fade slowly over time and much faster as the entity heals.
  • Water slowly washes dirt and damage marks off, only below the water line. Rain from open sky washes the whole body more slowly, and a water cauldron bath scrubs off every layer.
  • Honey and slime blocks smear the feet amber or green, faster than walking on dirt and even standing still. Sculk leaves teal-black stains. Sinking into powder snow coats whatever is under its surface in white, and lava slowly chars whatever is sunk in it.
  • Snowfall from open sky builds up on its own locked Snow layer, heaviest on the head and thinning out down the body in patches, under any damage marks. It melts once the snow stops.
  • Dirt and damage marks are real FFEntityPainting paint on their own locked layers, each with its own on/off switch and mob filters, so hand-painted art is never washed away.
  • Drips, on each player's own game: water and lava dripping from pools, pointed dripstone, wet sponges and rainy leaves land on mobs and players instead of falling through, leaving wet spots or scorch marks that fade. They are painted on FFEntityPainting local layers (Wet and Scorch) that only that player sees; client settings choose which particles paint and how.
  • Server settings for mob and block whitelists and blacklists (IDs, tags or whole mods), dirt colors, messiness, rates, reach and wash speed. Mob shapes are measured by FFEntityPainting, which has the measuring settings.
  • Datapack tags ffentitydirt:dirty_surfaces and ffentitydirt:clean_surfaces (blocks) ffentitydirt:never_dirty and ffentitydirt:never_damaged (entity types).

How it works in detail

  • Walking and running: every step on natural ground (soil, grass, sand, gravel, clay, mud, snow, stone and the like) has a good chance to leave a speck of dirt low on the body, with a little puff of ground particles at the feet. Wooden floors, wool, carpet and other indoor surfaces stay clean. The faster you go, the more often it happens and the higher it can reach, and a few stray flecks fly even higher. Standing still never adds dirt, and neither do swimming, riding or flying.
  • Colors come from the ground: grass leaves green, mud dark brown, sand sandy yellow, snow white. Blocks without a custom color use their map color, and every splat gets a little random shading so it looks speckled rather than flat.
  • Falls: fall damage slams dirt up the body from the bottom. The harder the landing, the more dirt and the higher it goes.
  • Mining: breaking stone, dirt or grass now and then flings a few specks of its color onto the player, on the side facing the block. Dig at your feet and your legs get grubby; dig overhead and the specks land on your head and arms.
  • Soot: catching fire, touching lava or standing in a campfire leaves soot, blackest at the feet and fading up to the head. It keeps building while you burn (up to a limit), even with Fire Resistance. The default soot is near black, so it stands out on light mobs and is subtler on dark ones; change soot.color for a bolder look.
  • Explosions: a blast leaves soot on the side facing it, creeping a little way around the sides. The closer you were, the darker the scorch. Hiding fully behind a wall keeps you clean, creative players get scorched too, and wind charges never leave soot. Soot darkens any dirt already there.
  • Damage marks: an arrow, trident or snowball leaves a wound where it hit. A melee hit leaves a wound where the attacker's line of sight from its eyes meets the body, scattered a little so repeated hits don't stack on one spot. When a mob looks just past its target, the line from its eyes to the nearest part of the body is used instead. With melee.aimFromEyes off, wounds land on the side facing the attacker at a random height, as before 0.8.0. Closer attackers leave bigger wounds, harder hits thicker and darker ones, and every wound goes on a little unevenly (damage.roughness).
  • Weapon wounds: what hits you decides the wound. Swords leave a slash, axes a thick gash, claymores, glaives, halberds and scythes a long cleave, spears, daggers and rapiers a stab with a drip running down, arrows and pickaxes a puncture, tridents three holes side by side, maces, hammers and iron golems a dark crater with spatter, fists, shovels and staffs a purplish bruise, hoes, shears and sickles two scratches, cats and polar bears three claw rakes, zombies, wolves and spiders a bite, hoglins two tusk tears, bees a sting, whips a lash, and llama spit and slimes a splatter. Weapons from other mods are matched by the wound tags, then by Better Combat's or Epic Fight's weapon type when one is installed, then by words in the item's name (a katana slashes, a warhammer crushes). Anything else with attack damage slashes and anything without bruises. The Datapacks page lists the tags.
  • Sticky blocks: standing or walking on honey or slime blocks smears your feet and ankles amber or green, much faster than walking on dirt and even while standing still. Bouncing in the air adds nothing.
  • Sculk: walking on sculk leaves teal-black stains, a bit messier than dirt.
  • Powder snow: sink into powder snow and whatever is under its surface slowly coats white, heaviest at the feet. It stays until washed off.
  • Lava: whatever is sunk in lava slowly chars dark, on top of the soot from burning, so a player with Fire Resistance wading through lava comes out with blackened legs. Naturally fire-immune mobs like striders stay uncharred.
  • Water: standing in water slowly fades dirt, soot, damage marks and snow, but only below the water line. Wading cleans your legs; a full swim cleans everything.
  • Rain: rain from open sky washes the whole body, more slowly than a swim. Any block overhead keeps you dry (and dirty).
  • Cauldron baths: sitting in a water cauldron (any fill level) slowly scrubs off every layer this mod adds.
  • Snow: snowfall from open sky piles up heaviest on the head and thins out down the body in uneven patches. It melts once you head inside or the snow stops, melts right off if you catch fire, and rain and water wash it off too.
  • Healing up: damage marks fade on their own over about ten minutes, and much faster as you heal. Back at full health, most marks are gone.
  • Outer layers: dirt and marks go on both a skin's outer layer (hat, jacket, sleeves, pants) and the body underneath, so they show whether that layer is see-through or solid.
  • Locked layers: dirt, damage marks and snow live on their own locked Dirt, Damage and Snow layers in the FFEntityPainting layer manager. You can see them but not paint on them, and washing never touches hand-painted layers. They sit above your own layers: dirt at the bottom, then snow, then damage marks. Each layer can be switched off on its own. Dirt is saved with the mob.
  • Drips (client only): water and lava dripping from pools, pointed dripstone, wet sponges and rainy leaves land on mobs and players instead of falling through. Water leaves dark wet spots that dry in about a minute and a half, lava leaves scorch marks that fade over ten minutes. They are painted on local FFEntityPainting layers called Wet and Scorch that only you see, stored on your computer until they fade, and they never wash off the server's dirt.
  • Water hose (debug item): a test toy for drip paint. /give @s ffentitydirt:water_hose, then hold use to spray a stream of water. Every mob it lands on gets the same local wet paint as drips, only bigger and faster. Other players see the stream, and each sees their own wet patches. It never paints you and needs the drip settings enabled.

Why do clients measure mob shapes?

Only a client knows what a mob's model looks like, so FFEntityPainting quietly measures nearby mobs now and then and tells the server where each paintable spot sits on the body. Mobs nobody has seen yet don't pick up dirt or marks until a player comes near. Marks land on the measured shape, so they are spot on for heads and bodies and can be a few pixels off on swinging arms and legs. Measuring settings are part of FFEntityPainting's config.

Configuration

Dirt, damage, snow and cleaning are server rules shared with every player; drips are each player's own client settings. Every option, its default and what it does is on the Configuration page.

Good to know

  • Dirt and damage marks are paint, so they share the mob's paint budget with hand-painted art.
  • If you're painting a mob by hand at the exact moment dirt is written, your click is refused and you just click again. Raise flushIntervalTicks if this bugs you.
  • Drowning, poison and thorns don't leave marks yet. Fall damage makes dirt, and fire and explosions make soot, instead of marks.
  • Mining specks only land on players, since only players break blocks.
  • Armor, held items and mobs with Entity Model Features custom models don't show dirt, same as FFEntityPainting.
  • Drips need particles to be on. With Particles: Minimal most drips never appear. Spots only fade while the mob is within maxDistance.
  • Lava drips only leave marks; they don't set anything on fire.

Datapack tags are covered on the Datapacks page.
