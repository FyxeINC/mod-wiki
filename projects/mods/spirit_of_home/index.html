The Spirit of Home

Modrinth ↗CurseForge ↗Source ↗

Overview

Every home deserves a spirit. Place a Cornerstone to meet a randomly named, brightly colored home spirit, automatically attune to it and receive a chat greeting with a swirl of colored particles.

Crafting

Use a bone in the top center, a stone slab in the middle center and three ordinary stones across the bottom row. Produces one Cornerstone; acquiring a bone unlocks its recipe-book entry. It also appears in the Building Blocks Creative tab.

Craft a Spirit Totem with a bone above two logs in a vertical column. It drops itself, is mined with an axe, and appears in Building Blocks with a recipe-book unlock from bone. Its wooden-and-bone shelter pauses spiritless buildup while travelling; it does not reset absence, award welcomes, grant home benefits or change respawns.

Placing and mining

Placement automatically replaces your current attunement. Shift-right-click with any held item to attune to another stone; repeating the same attunement is refused. A player can be attuned to only one spirit. Bonds persist through logout, death and dimension travel, but only function in the stone's dimension and while its chunk is loaded.

Pistons cannot push, pull or destroy it. Survival mining takes 30 seconds by default regardless of tools, effects or environment, and returns one bone, one stone slab and three stones. Creative mining is instant with no drops. Breaking a Cornerstone tells the breaker: “You feel slightly colder. [CORNERSTONE NAME] is gone...” Replacement stones have new identities, new names, new colors and zero experience; they do not inherit old attunements.

Death respawns prefer your attuned Cornerstone over your bed, including in another dimension. The respawn check loads the bonded chunk and verifies the spirit identity, then looks for safe, dry, collision-free space beside or above the stone. Keep space clear. Missing, replaced or completely blocked homes use normal spawning. Your bed is retained for those cases; returning through the End exit portal is unchanged. Ordinary aura and totem checks never load chunks.

Level and screen

Right-click to view the spirit's experience, level and activity meters. Color editing unlocks at Cornerstone level 2 and naming at level 3 by default, with configurable minimum levels. The warm stone screen sizes to its content, with spirit identity and XP above a compact activity ring on the left and Home Bonuses on the right. The bonus list shows unlocked home attributes, hunger exhaustion savings, healing and hostile spawn protection for the current spirit level, including datapack definitions. Amounts are shown at full aura strength for an attuned player; hover the heading for distance and spawn-protection conditions. Longer lists wrap and scroll within the viewport. Click the color swatch or pencil to open a labeled editor with Save and Cancel, inline validation and a live hex color preview. Unavailable controls are disabled and gray without padlocks or adjacent unlock numbers; hover tooltips explain the requirement. Escape cancels editing first; Enter saves valid input. Tab and Shift+Tab move between the input and buttons, and drafts survive resizing. The five home categories appear as a compact radial group of item silhouettes: bed, crafting table, bread, door and sword. These flat, borderless item silhouettes fill from the bottom in each category's configured color, with neutral empty portions. Custom categories use a shard silhouette. The fixed silhouettes do not use resource-pack item models. The Home Activities heading over the left column sits below the compact player-style XP bar with the green cornerstone level centered over it, including level 0; exact XP appears only in the tooltip. A green arrow beside the bar appears whenever any category has stored progress above zero. Only category names are colored in their tooltips; percentages and hints stay neutral. Press Escape to close the menu; there is no Done button. Names, percentages and action hints appear only in mouse or keyboard tooltips. All five stay on one page on smaller viewports, where the group flattens to an oval; extra datapack categories use additional pages. Page controls appear only when needed. Shortened names have an ellipsis; tooltips retain full names and exact XP values. Any player within interaction reach can edit unlocked fields; names must be nonblank printable text up to 100 characters and colors must be six RGB hex digits (for example #FF8000). The server verifies the open menu, original stone, reach, levels and values. New spirits start with a random fully saturated, bright color. XP-driven level increases celebrate with spirit-colored dust, happy-villager particles and a level-up sound. Lowering the level locks editing again without discarding customization.

Levels start at 0 and are derived from saved experience, capped at 100. Moving from level L to L+1 costs the rounded-up result of experienceEquation, default base * (level + 1)^exponent. Variables are level (current level), base (experienceBase) and exponent (experienceExponent). Costs accumulate: defaults require total 100 XP for level 1, 500 for level 2, 1400 for level 3. Removing experience can lower a level. Changing equation settings recalculates levels from current XP. Equations support +, -, *, /, %, ^, parentheses, abs, min, max, clamp, pow, sqrt, floor, ceil and round. Invalid syntax or unknown variables are rejected. Nonfinite/nonpositive results fall back to 100 * (level+1)^2; each cost clamps to 1..92233720368547758 to keep thresholds increasing. Surplus XP beyond level 100 remains stored. Experience comes from home category decay, homesick returns and operator commands. The screen shows a horizontal vanilla green XP bar with the current level and progress toward the next level; level 100 keeps a full bar.

Home categories

Rest, Craft, Board, Shelter and Vigil each store a value from 0 to 1. Rest rewards sleep and completed music discs. Craft rewards ordinary crafting, smelting, repairing, enchanting, brewing, disenchanting, smithing and stonecutting. Board rewards eating, cooking, mature harvests, milk, honey and tree growth. Shelter rewards building, stripping logs, waxing copper and creating paths. Vigil rewards defeating monsters and completing zombie-villager cures. Every action has its own datapack weight: ordinary crafting is the lowest workshop gain (0.01), while placing blocks gives 0.002, less than maintenance. See the complete defaults and trigger table. Spectator player actions do not contribute. Category icons fill with stored activity; hover or focus for names, percentages and action hints.

Actions fill every loaded Cornerstone whose spherical radius contains the action location, with no distance falloff. Player workshop actions, sleeping and eating use the player's position; maintenance, harvesting, collection and kills use the block or entity position. Environmental music, brewing, campfire cooking and tree growth reward once at their block location, using the gain equation at level 0 and full contribution, regardless of nearby player count. A completed cure uses the curing player when online in the same dimension, otherwise environmental scaling. Each matching action first evaluates actionGainEquation with amount (datapack gain) and level (the player's current Minecraft XP level). Default: amount * (1 + level / 30); a level-30 player sleeping contributes 0.8 Rest instead of 0.4. Nonfinite or negative results fall back to that formula. Then attuned players contribute 100%; all other players use nonAttunedMultiplier, default 50% (0.4 Rest in the example). Values clamp at 1 and excess is lost. See Datapacks for category definitions, disabling and action filters.

Each meter decays linearly by categoryDecayAmount per categoryDecayIntervalTicks. Only the value actually consumed produces XP: consumed value × experiencePerCategoryUnit. Defaults consume 0.01 per minute while any nonspectator player is inside this home, awarding 1 XP; 0.2 consumed awards 20 XP. Fractional XP persists. With nobody inside the sphere, awayDecayMultiplier (default 2) doubles consumption and XP conversion per tick, bounded by stored values. Presence changes settle the preceding interval at its original rate; unload settles before saving. A reloaded chunk catches up at the away rate using elapsed world game ticks, capped by the stored value, without loading chunks to do so. Time while the server is stopped and skipped day/night time do not count. Removing a category or disabling it discards its value without XP on the next decay update. Config changes apply current decay settings to outstanding elapsed time.

Additive home benefits

Only your attuned spirit supplies benefits. Other overlapping Cornerstones have no effect on you. Level 0 grants movement speed; level 1 also adds growing mining speed, block reach and food exhaustion savings; level 2 adds growing home healing and suppresses hostile mob spawns. Higher levels improve unlocked benefits. Datapacks independently control the start level and growth curve of each buff.

Radius is radiusEquation, default distance * (level + 1), where distance is radiusPerLevel; the default datapack movement bonus is 0.1 * (level + 1) * strength. Level 0 defaults to 8 blocks and +10% movement speed. Level 1+ adds +20% mining speed and 0.25 blocks of reach per level. Hunger exhaustion savings are 1 - 1/(1+0.1*level) from level 1. Level 2+ heals 0.1*(level-1) health points per second. These home bonuses stay full through half the spherical radius and fade linearly to zero at its outer edge. Distance includes vertical separation. The Cornerstone itself always retains its fixed mining duration.

Level 2+ spawn protection covers the sphere only while a nonspectator attuned player is inside it. It blocks hostile Enemy spawns through NeoForge position/finalization hooks, including spawners, while leaving existing mobs and passive animals alone. Command and spawn-egg summons are exempt. Modded spawning that bypasses these hooks is not covered.

Updates run every 5 server ticks. Leaving range, unloading the stone, removing it or changing dimensions clears the temporary modifiers and HUD; unrelated modifiers are preserved. Spectators do not receive benefits or activate protection. No chunks are force-loaded.

Homesickness and homecoming

Spiritless persists from 0 to 1 across logout, death and dimension changes. It increases every five online server ticks while you are outside every loaded Cornerstone sphere. The default duration is 72000 ticks (one hour at 20 TPS) from 0 to 1. Any loaded Cornerstone or Spirit Totem within totemRadius (default 8 blocks, inclusive spherical distance from block center) pauses growth; only your attuned home clears it. Offline time does not count, and spectators neither accumulate spiritless nor receive penalties or return rewards. No chunks are force-loaded.

At spiritless 0.3 by default, you are homesick. Penalty severity is clamp((spiritless - homesickThreshold) / (1 - homesickThreshold), 0, 1): zero at the threshold, maximum at 1. The default mining penalty scales to 50% and attack-speed penalty to 30%; datapacks can add movement or other attribute penalties. Additional food exhaustion scales to 0.4 per second, using vanilla hunger rules (Creative and Peaceful are exempt). These independent temporary attribute modifiers preserve unrelated modifiers. Cornerstones retain their fixed mining duration.

At raw spiritless 0.75 (75%) by default, homesick Survival players cannot break or place ordinary blocks. The threshold is independent of penalty severity and requires homesickness. Creative and spectators are exempt. The spirit_of_home:adventure_blocks block tag allows travel supplies such as torches, ladders, grass/plants and beds, plus Cornerstones and Spirit Totems for recovery. Each operation can be disabled separately. Totems and other homes pause buildup but do not clear existing restrictions; returning to your attuned home clears them. Blocked actions show an action-bar reminder. Multi-block placements require every placed block to be allowed; otherwise the whole placement is rolled back. See Adventure blocks for custom and modded exceptions.

Return inside your attuned home's radius to clear spiritless. If homesick, you also receive player XP points and your spirit gains XP, with a spirit-colored “Welcome home” chat message and small particles on both you and the stone. Default rewards are 100 * spiritless^2 * (1 + 0.1 * level) player XP and 200 * spiritless^2 * (1 + 0.1 * level) spirit XP, rounded to the nearest point. At Cornerstone level 0, spiritless 0.5 gives 25 and 50 XP; spiritless 1 gives 100 and 200. Each additional home level adds 10% of those base rewards. The equations use pre-reset spiritless and the Cornerstone level after pending category decay but before either XP reward, reject invalid syntax/unknown variables, fall back to these defaults for negative/nonfinite results, and cap each reward at 1000000. An experience-bearing welcome grants +10% movement and +20% mining times (level+1), lasting 600+20*level ticks. Regeneration lasts 200+20*level ticks and rises from I at levels 0–9 to II at 10–19, up to V from level 40. Buffs use the post-reward level. Datapacks control these buffs, durations and independent unlock levels. Existing worlds retain saved XP equations until edited; new defaults apply to newly generated configs. Clearing absence before rewards prevents repeated welcomes while you stay home. Returns below the threshold clear absence without rewards.

Permission level 2 is required for these commands. For info, experience and category commands, look directly at a Cornerstone within block interaction reach (capped at 64); solid obstructions reject the target. Commands update nearby benefits and open screens. Give/remove accepts positive whole amounts up to 9223372036854775807; additions saturate there, removals stop at zero. Category commands suggest active resource ids, accept amounts from 0 to 1 and clamp results to that range. They settle pending decay first; direct command removal does not generate XP. Direct level commands have been removed.

/cornerstone experience get
/cornerstone experience give 500
/cornerstone experience remove 100

/cornerstone spiritless add PlayerName 0.1
/cornerstone spiritless remove PlayerName 0.2

Spiritless commands require permission level 2, accept one online player name or single-player selector, and work from the console without looking at a stone. Get reports the value and homesick status. Set/add/remove take an amount from 0 to 1 and clamp the resulting value to that range. Penalties refresh immediately; the next aura update handles a return if the player is already home.

Home HUD

Within your attuned home's radius, the HUD displays its name and level in its spirit color. It starts at the top center with an 8-pixel downward offset, respects the hide-GUI option and disappears outside the aura. Each player's config/spirit_of_home-client.toml controls the homeHud group in scaled GUI pixels:

Homesick HUD

While homesick, a small house icon and “Homesick: N%” show your raw spiritless value (0–1 converted to a rounded percent). It defaults to top center with an 8-pixel downward offset. The icon and text change from amber to red at homesickBuildingThreshold, even when the breaking/placement restrictions are disabled. They use 50% opacity while a loaded Spirit Totem or another Cornerstone pauses buildup; penalties continue. F1 hides it. The separate homesickHud config group has enabled=true, horizontal=MIDDLE, vertical=TOP, offsetX=0 and offsetY=8, with the same anchor choices and offset ranges as homeHud. Disabling the home HUD does not disable this one.

Datapacks

Customize home/homesick attribute formulas, healing, exhaustion savings and welcome buffs, replace or extend spirit name/descriptor pools and activity categories, and override crafting recipes, ingredient drops and mining tags. See Datapacks for paths and examples.

Compatibility

Install on both server and client. No special integrations or public API. Piston immunity covers vanilla pistons; other mods forcibly relocating blocks are outside that mechanic.

Requirements and source version

Source version: 0.8.0. Currently targets Minecraft 1.21.1, NeoForge 21.1.211 or newer and Java 21. FFUtilities 0.2.12 is bundled.
